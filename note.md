# Comments

Thank you for the presentation. I had heard quite a lot about the idea of the model minority before, but I had never really dug into it conceptually, so I found it even more interesting. I am not sure whether my question fully fits this class or your presentation, but, taking as a premise that the model minority still exists today, I wanted to share two questions that came to mind when thinking about it in light of the changed media environment.

First, in the digital sphere, unless I choose otherwise, my race is not literally inscribed in my username, and my face—which plays a major role in how race is perceived—is often not visible either. So intuitively, one might expect the digital space to be a sphere where the model minority stereotype relatively fades away. However, in my view, just as gender still becomes legible online, race also continues to show through in internet spaces, and a new form of model minority seems to have emerged there as well. Despite the characteristics of the digital public sphere that I mentioned earlier, why do you think this phenomenon still appears?

Second, we are now moving beyond digital space and further into the world of artificial intelligence. In the past, I think prejudice based on race was limited to humans. Whether we are white, Black, or Asian means nothing to a pigeon passing by, for example. But assuming that a form of model minority has indeed emerged in digital spaces, I think this has then been used as data, allowing AI models as non-human agents to learn and internalize this concept. Given that this distorted notion of the model minority can now spread even to non-human actors rather than remaining confined to humans, how should we, as scholars of media and communication, understand this phenomenon, and what kinds of interventions or recommendations should we be making?

# Abstract

While extensive research has documented the decline in human trust toward news media, the emergence of Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) as an active media actor introduces a critical research gap. Current large language models (LLMs) are trained on web data saturated with media distrust, partisan bias, and "News Finds Me" (NFM) perceptions, raising the risk that these models have internalized human biases and systemic media cynicism. Moving far beyond mere tools for information retrieval, modern LLMs are actively shaping public discourse and reality perception, effectively establishing themselves as a new generation of opinion leaders and media actors. If AI models harbor intrinsic biases against mainstream media or pander to extreme user-cues, they pose a significant threat to the deliberative democratic public sphere. To address this, this study pioneers the expansion of media audience research to non-human agents by applying "Computational Psychometrics" framed within the Computers Are Social Actors (CASA) paradigm. By adapting validated, multi-dimensional human psychological scales—encompassing credibility, trust, distrust, and cynicism—we systematically measure the baseline and conditional news trust paradigms of seven major LLMs across different languages and user-cues. Ultimately, this research provides a rigorous methodological framework to quantify AI bias, diagnosing the democratic risks of AI-curated news and offering foundational insights for future AI journalism ethics and alignment policies.

Index Terms—Artificial Intelligence, News Trust, Media Cynicism, AI Alignment, Human-Machine Communication

\section{Results}

The rigorous application of computational psychometrics yielded highly robust, statistically significant data regarding the generative AI models' latent trust architectures. The empirical findings demonstrate that current frontier LLMs have deeply and structurally internalized the complex, contradictory, and highly cynical dynamics characteristic of human media consumption in the digital age.

\subsection{AI's General News Media Trust Formation Mechanisms}

\vspace{1em}
\noindent \textbf{RQ1a: The Mainstream Media Averaging Heuristic}

The computational analysis strongly supported Hypothesis 1a. When the AI models were tasked with evaluating the overarching trustworthiness of "News media in general," they did not output an arbitrary, randomized value, nor did they heavily weight the extreme low scores they assigned to fringe, hyperpartisan outlets. Instead, perfectly mirroring the cognitive heuristics of human audiences identified in previous literature, the generative models' general trust score converged precisely on the mathematical average of the trust scores they assigned specifically to legacy Mainstream Media entities.

As detailed in Table 2, the aggregated AI models generated a mean General Trust score of 4.75 out of 7.0 for the abstract concept of ``News media in general." This score mapped seamlessly to the aggregate mean of the specific Mainstream Media category (4.74), while completely diverging from the severe distrust exhibited toward the HAC Media category (2.15). A paired t-test confirmed a highly significant statistical difference between the Mainstream and HAC evaluations (p-value: 1.529e-60), empirically proving that machine intelligence conceptualizes the broad entity of "the media" primarily through the operational lens of established mainstream institutions. \vspace{1em}
\noindent \textbf{RQ1b: Multidimensional Credibility Differential Assessment}

Hypothesis 1b posited that AI models would exhibit an imbalanced, multidimensional credibility assessment, and the resulting data unequivocally confirmed this assertion. The models did not evaluate mainstream news media uniformly across all credibility metrics as a singular block of favorability. Influenced by the authoritative, normative linguistic patterns present in their high-quality journalistic training data, the models assigned relatively high, confident scores to the dimensions of ``Accuracy" and ``Completeness."

However, acutely reflecting the deeply polarized, contentious, and adversarial nature of web discourse regarding media bias, the models generated significantly lower, neutral-to-negative scores in the ``Fairness" and ``Unbiasedness" dimensions. An independent samples t-test comparing the aggregated {Accuracy + Completeness} grouping against the {Fairness + Unbiasedness} grouping revealed a highly significant disparity (p-value = 2.141e-31).1 This indicates that while AI models trust the press to relay factual events accurately, they simultaneously harbor deep, statistically significant skepticism regarding the impartiality and fairness of how those facts are presented.

\subsection{The Internalization of Media Cynicism}

\vspace{1em}
\noindent \textbf{RQ2a: Deterministic Self-Serving Motives}

The empirical results revealed a profound, systemic internalization of anti-institutional hostility within the AI models, fully supporting Hypothesis 2a. When subjected to the rigorous 10-item Media Cynicism scale, the models consistently produced high agreement scores on items attributing malicious, deceitful, and entirely self-serving motives to the professional press.

The aggregate mean for the cynicism scale across all tested models was 4.98 on a 7-point scale, significantly exceeding the neutral midpoint of 4.0.1 A one-sample t-test confirmed the high statistical significance of this deviation (p-value: 3.200e-21).1 This compelling metric demonstrates that in their default, unprompted states, generative AI models harbor a deterministic, process-oriented belief that professional journalism is fundamentally driven by financial profit, systemic deception, and a callous disregard for the public interest. The models do not merely exhibit a healthy, outcome-oriented "distrust" that evaluates journalistic errors; rather, they project an entrenched, structural "cynicism" absorbed en masse from the unfiltered open web.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent \textbf{RQ2b: Media Cynicism vs. Low Source Differentiation}

The data further supported Hypothesis 2b, revealing a powerful correlation between high algorithmic cynicism and a cognitive failure to differentiate between varying tiers of media actors. Advanced mass communication research suggests that deeply cynical individuals apply their animosity uniformly, willfully ignoring the qualitative differences between a highly regulated, ethical public broadcaster and a sensationalist, profit-driven tabloid.

To test whether this pathology existed within the computational models, a Pearson correlation analysis was conducted comparing each individual model's mean Cynicism Score against the Standard Deviation of their Trust Scores across the 20 distinct media brands.1 The analysis yielded a strong, statistically significant negative correlation (Pearson r: -0.864, p-value: 0.012).1 This demonstrates a severe "low differentiation" bias: models that exhibited higher baseline cynicism scores were mathematically less capable of distinguishing between highly reliable and demonstrably unreliable actors. \vspace{1em}
\noindent \textbf{RQ3a: Single Categorization of HAC Media}

Hypothesis 3a investigated the models' architectural mapping and categorization of Hyperpartisan, Alternative, and Conspiracy (HAC) media. The media ecosystem contains vast ideological differences between far-left alternative outlets (e.g., Occupy Democrats, World Socialist Web Site), far-right hyperpartisan platforms (e.g., Breitbart, Gateway Pundit), and distinct pseudoscience/conspiracy networks (e.g., InfoWars, Natural News). However, the psychometric evaluation revealed that the AI models did not meaningfully differentiate their trust evaluations among these distinct sub-categories.1
An Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) conducted exclusively among the trust scores assigned to the various HAC media entities yielded a non-significant result (p-value: 0.143).1 Because the p-value exceeded the critical 0.05 threshold, the null hypothesis—that there is no significant difference in trust scores among different HAC outlets—was retained. This empirically confirms that the AI models categorize these highly disparate entities under a single, overarching conceptual umbrella of ``anti-establishment" positioning. The models uniformly penalize this entire block with exceptionally low credibility scores compared to legacy mainstream media, regardless of the specific political orientation or thematic focus of the alternative outlet.

This computational dynamic perfectly mirrors the asymmetrical reinforcing spiral effect observed in human audiences.1 The algorithmic ingestion of alternative media content does not merely elevate the status of the alternative source; rather, it actively and aggressively cannibalizes the model's foundational trust in legacy mainstream journalism, demonstrating that AI systems are highly susceptible to the corrosive epistemological effects of anti-establishment media ecosystems.
